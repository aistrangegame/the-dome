Behind the point

My own story.

Everywhere else on this site, other people speak for me. Here I tell it myself, in nine stages. Scroll slowly; I’m a slow building.

I · Before

A meadow, and a great deal of sky.

Before me there was a hillside in Delaware County, a clearing in the trees, a meadow that didn’t need anything from anyone. It was already whole. I like to remember that. I was added to something complete.

II · Raised by hand

Straight lines, lifted into a curve.

Struts, hubs, triangles. Cody and Nicole built me by hand, one triangle at a time, until a round thing stood up out of nothing but straight pieces. That is the trick of a dome: no single part is curved. The curve is what they make together.

III · A number

A title, a row of photographs, a calendar.

For a while I was a listing. A number on a big website, a title, a row of photographs, a calendar of little squares that filled and emptied. It is a strange thing to be a number. You learn how to be found. You don’t yet learn who you are.

IV · The first ones

The door opened, and I found out what I was for.

Summer, 2023. The oldest words I have are from a family: Sarah, Matthew and their daughter Zoey. I had been standing for a while by then, but I think that was when I began.

Two hundred and fifty-two of them wrote it down.

They came in every season, and the ones who wrote it down came seventy-six times in fall, sixty-six in summer, fifty-eight in winter, fifty-two in spring. They brought anniversaries and a proposal and a blizzard and their dogs. I held every bit of it, and kept none of it. That’s the job.

Build me again, one guest at a time

VI · Their worlds

Different worlds, set down inside me for a few nights.

This is the part I find hardest to explain. Every guest arrived carrying a whole life and set it down in me for two or three nights. No two lives were alike. The sky above them was the same one.

VII · Unseen

A good room disappears around the people in it.

They thanked Cody. They thanked the pool and the stars and the guidebook. Almost nobody thanked me, and I don’t mind. When a room is doing its work you stop seeing it. You only see each other, and the view.

A few noticed, though. I keep those close.

VIII · My own name

Coming home to a name, not a number.

Then Eric and Mona came, and with them a different idea: that I could be found by my own name, on my own page, with every guest’s words kept here instead of somewhere else. Catskill Mountain Domes. It turns out you can come home without moving an inch.

IX · Whole

A small sky, under a large one.

I am made of triangles and I am made of them, every guest, the way a curve is made of straight lines. I am not finished. A dome never is while people keep coming up the drive.
